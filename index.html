About

I am a Lecturer in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at North South University in Dhaka, Bangladesh, where I teach pattern recognition and neural networks, algorithms, and digital logic design.

My research is in medical AI: building machine learning methods that turn physiological and clinical data into information that supports diagnosis and care. I have worked on detecting fetal movement from a wearable abdominal monitor in the Wellcome Leap In Utero program, screening for autism spectrum disorder from eye-tracking data, detecting physical fatigue from wearable sensors, and recognizing activities from wearable and vision sensors for assistive healthcare. A research grant from North South University funded my recent work on mathematical modeling of human activity recognition for healthcare.

I am especially interested in biomedical signal analysis, medical imaging, and wearable health monitoring, and in making these models reliable enough to use in clinical settings.

I hold an MSc in Human Factors Engineering from the University at Buffalo, The State University of New York, and a BSc in Robotics and Mechatronics Engineering from the University of Dhaka.

Projects

Autistic child evaluation through eye-movement analysis

An automated early-screening method for autism spectrum disorder. Saliency maps built from raw eye-tracking data are classified to tell observer groups apart by their gaze patterns. Extended from my undergraduate thesis into an ICPR paper.

Eye tracking, saliency maps, machine learning

Medication management interface for older adults

A UX design for an app that helps older adults with chronic conditions keep track of their medications, with zoom, screen reader and voice input, adjustable text and display modes, and appointment reminders.

UX design, accessibility

Pipe inspection crawling robot using reinforcement learning

A crawling robot that pulls itself forward with a two-degree-of-freedom arm and learns to adapt to pipes of different diameters and materials using Q-learning.

Reinforcement learning, Q-learning, robotics

Interbotix robotic arm path planning in ROS

A simulated pick-and-place task in a custom Gazebo world. The arm lifts stationary and movable objects, moves them to target locations, and detects object colors.

ROS, Gazebo, MoveIt

Optimal shelf-fetching routes in a warehouse

A prototype puzzle-based storage and retrieval system modeled on the 15-puzzle: eight TurtleBots stand in for shelves in a 3 × 3 grid, and the planner retrieves a target shelf along the optimal path.

ROS, Gazebo, path planning

IoT poultry farm monitoring device

Monitors temperature, humidity and ammonia in incubators and hatchers, alerts farmers when a reading leaves the safe range, and keeps historical data in the cloud.

IoT, embedded systems, sensors

PID-controlled line follower robot

A line follower that tracks a black line under varying lighting and surface textures, with the control algorithm, hardware and calibration built from scratch. It reached a 95% success rate in testing.

C++, PID control, circuit design
